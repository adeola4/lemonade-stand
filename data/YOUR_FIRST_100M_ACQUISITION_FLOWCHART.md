## PHASE 1: TARGET SOURCING & IDENTIFICATION

### 1.1 DEFINE ACQUISITION CRITERIA

> **"Find an industry in chaos, going through [change]"** — E119
> **"Quantum Leap success means fishing with nets, not just with lines"** — M011

**Ideal Target Profile:**
- Fragmented industry (many small competitors)
- Dominated by mom-and-pop companies
- Large enough to matter
- EBIT ~$25,000+ minimum
- Manufacturing or distribution (preferred over service)
- Industry in chaos or transition — *"From chaos comes order. In chaos is opportunity — Chaordic!"* (M035)
- No one else consolidating (yet)
- Area of existing expertise — *"Stick to your knitting"* (PI021)

### 1.3 THE INQUIRY LETTER (Initial Outreach)

> **"We are interested in acquiring a [type] firm located within the greater [area], with sales ranging between $X and $Y. Our preference is for a company that [criteria]. We are principals (not business brokers) with adequate capital and financing, and we're ready to move quickly. All matters held in strict confidence."** — E979-985

**Key Elements:**
1. State you're a **principal** (not a broker)
2. Confirm **adequate capital** and financing
3. Express ability to **move quickly**
4. Promise **strict confidentiality**
5. Specify **geography** and **size range**
6. Include a **call to action**

## PHASE 3: NON-DISCLOSURE AGREEMENT (NDA)

### 3.1 SENDING THE NDA

> **"Say something like, 'This is pretty standard. We always do this to protect both parties. Hell, sometimes I even sign one myself.'"** — D173

**NDA Content Checklist:**
| # | Element | Detail |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | **Definition of Confidential Information** | Financials, customer lists, employee data, trade secrets, operations |
| 2 | **Exclusions** | Publicly available info, independently developed, disclosed by third party |
| 3 | **Obligations** | Use ONLY for evaluating the transaction |
| 4 | **Term** | How long confidentiality lasts (typically 2-5 years) |
| 5 | **Return/Destruction** | Must return or destroy documents if deal doesn't close |
| 6 | **No License** | No IP rights transferred |
| 7 | **Remedies** | Injunctive relief available |
| 8 | **Non-Solicitation** | Can't poach employees/customers if deal fails |
| 9 | **Governing Law** | Which state's law applies |
| 10 | **Counterparts** | Can sign in separate copies |

**Delivery Process:**
1. Send NDA via email (PDF) with cover note
2. Normalize: "This is pretty standard. We always do this to protect both parties."
3. Follow up within 48 hours if no response
4. Both parties sign → return fully executed copy
5. **START THE CLOCK** — document request goes out same day

## PHASE 5: TERM SHEET / INDICATIVE OFFER

### 5.1 VALUATION METHODS

> **"A good rule to remember is that if cash flow will not service the debt, you're paying too much!"** — E1028

> **"Companies are valued by the market, not by what the seller needs."** — E1089-E1093

| Method | When to Use | Multiple/Formula |
|--------|-------------|------------------|
| **Cash Flow Multiple** | Stable, profitable companies | **3-5x annual cash flow** |
| **Gross Revenue Multiple** | "Cash cows" or high-growth | 0.5-2x annual revenue |
| **Asset-Based** | Asset-heavy businesses | Book value or adjusted net asset value |
| **Market Comparison** | Public company comps available | P/E ratio or EV/EBITDA |
| **DCF** | Predictable future cash flows | Discounted future cash flows |

### 5.2 TERM SHEET CONTENT

| # | Term | Description |
|---|------|-------------|

| 2 | **Structure** | Stock purchase vs. asset purchase |

| 4 | **Cash at Closing** | Amount paid on closing date |
| 5 | **Seller Note** | Amount financed by seller, terms |
| 6 | **Earn-Out** | Contingent payments based on performance |
| 7 | **Working Capital Adjustment** | True-up mechanism |
| 8 | **Exclusivity Period** | No-shop clause (60-90 days) |

## PHASE 6: LETTER OF INTENT (LOI)

### 6.1 LOI vs. CONTRACT

> **"I don't use contracts, because a letter of intent is just as effective and less intimidating to the seller."** — E1025

**Why LOI:**
- Less intimidating to seller
- Faster to negotiate
- Still covers all key terms
- Binding only on exclusivity and expenses
- Allows for due diligence before final commitment

### 6.2 LOI CONTENT (Detailed)

| Section | Content |
|---------|---------|
| **1. Parties** | Buyer and seller legal names |
| **2. Transaction Description** | Stock purchase / asset purchase / merger |
| **3. Purchase Price** | Total consideration |
| **4. Payment Structure** | Cash, seller note, earn-out, holdback |
| **5. Working Capital Target** | Required working capital at closing |
| **6. Due Diligence** | Scope and timeline |
| **7. Exclusivity** | No-shop clause (60-90 days) |
| **8. Closing Conditions** | Financing, regulatory, consents |
| **9. Covenants** | Operations between signing and closing |
| **10. Break-Up Fee** | If seller accepts another offer |
| **11. Binding Provisions** | Exclusivity, confidentiality, expenses |
| **12. Expiration** | LOI expires if not signed by X date |
| **13. Governing Law** | Which state's law applies |
| **14. Signatures** | Both parties |

### 6.3 LOI NEGOTIATION POINTS

| Point | Seller Wants | Buyer Wants | Compromise |
|-------|--------------|-------------|------------|
| **Price** | Higher | Lower | Market multiple + earn-out |
| **Cash at Closing** | More cash | Less cash | Split difference |
| **Seller Note** | None or short-term | Long-term, low interest | 3-5 years, market rate |
| **Earn-Out** | Higher, easier targets | Lower, harder targets | Tie to EBITDA or revenue |
| **Exclusivity** | Shorter (30 days) | Longer (90 days) | 60 days |
| **Working Capital** | Higher target | Lower target | Historical average |
| **Break-Up Fee** | None | 2-4% of price | 1-2% of price |

### 8.3 KEY NEGOTIATION POINTS

|-------|--------------|-------------|

## PHASE 9: NEGOTIATION

### 9.1 NEGOTIATION FUNDAMENTALS

> **"Negotiation is a poker game, played with fortunes at stake instead of chips. To win — and this is key — you must make the other guys think they want what you've got more than you want what they've got."** — D135-136

> **"Knowledge of your foe is your most powerful weapon."** — D138

> **"Negotiating, like super success itself, is not for the touchy-feely."** — D133

### 9.2 THE POWER POSITIONING

| Tactic | Principle | Source |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| **Home Court** | Negotiate at YOUR office — "with the ghosts of past victories won there... adversaries are on unfamiliar ground. They are dependent on me and my staff for coffee and other amenities, and to tell them where the restrooms are — if I decide to tell them." | D142-145 |
| **Dress** | Suits required — "A clown in a sport shirt tells me he's got something else on his mind." | D140-141 |
| **Contract** | YOU draft the contract | D146-148 |
| **Nobody Leaves** | "Nobody leaves until the deal is done. Tell counterparts in advance... Buzz your assistant: 'No one is accepting calls. Turn off cell phones.' Have food thoughtfully ordered in. Conference rooms should have adjoining restrooms." | D149-163 |
| **Decision Maker** | Must be present or no meeting — "If one executive is 'not able' to attend... Either they don't want to make a final decision, or a key player is revealing an attitude problem." | D151-157 |
| **Comfort Zone** | "Every party has a comfort zone. The effective negotiator is the one who gets the other party OUT of their comfort zone." | D025, C126-128 |

### 9.3 THE WALKOUT SCRIPT

> **"If you can't agree to the point in the deal, say so. 'This proposal is unacceptable to me.' [Then stand up.] Never, NEVER look back. They're watching you from the windows. Then you disappear from their day. A dramatic exit is a killer because it's always unexpected. The deal can always resurrect another day."** — D175-179

**The Walkout Technique:**
1. Stand up firmly
2. Say: **"This proposal is unacceptable to me."**
3. Walk out — **NEVER look back**
4. They WILL watch you from the windows
5. Disappear from their day
6. The deal can always be resurrected later
7. A dramatic exit is devastating because it's unexpected

## PHASE 10: FINANCING & FUNDING

### 10.1 CAPITAL SOURCING

> **"There is more money out there crying to be borrowed than there are people willing to borrow it."** — C001

> **"A 'no' really means, 'You haven't given us sufficient reason to say yes.'"** — C057

**Capital Sources:**

| Source | When to Use | Key Terms |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| **Commercial Banks** | Asset-heavy deals, proven cash flow | Prime + 1-3%, 5-7 year term |
| **SBA Loans** | Smaller deals (<$5M) | Lower rates, government guarantee |
| **Seller Notes** | Seller financing | 8-10%, interest-only 5-7 years |
| **Mezzanine Debt** | Between equity and senior debt | 12-15%, often with equity kickers |
| **Private Equity** | Larger deals, growth capital | 20-30% ownership |
| **Venture Capital** | High-growth, early stage | Significant ownership, board seats |
| **Angel Investors** | Smaller amounts, individual | 10-25% ownership |
| **Public Offering** | Large companies, exit | Expensive (10-20% fees), loses control |

### 10.3 THE BANKER MEETING

**Rules:**
1. Take NO financial info to first meeting (C021)
2. Let banker talk about himself (C020)
3. Practice your pitch; don't stutter (C013)
4. Ask for 3-5x more than you think you need (E894-896)
5. Worry about getting the loan, NOT the rate (E897)
6. Never mention doubts to your banker (C0809)
7. End of year = best time (75% loans in Q4) (E905)
8. Write your business plan like your deal is going to change forever (C040)

**What to Say:**
> **"Write it like your deal is going to change forever [to punch it up]. Even if they see your touch of hype for what it is, they'll appreciate it as an expression of your [confidence]."** — C040

**What NOT to Say:**
> **"If you don't have an answer, never try to BS your way through. Chances are that they're asking some questions they already know the answers to, just to check [your integrity]."** — C055

**If you don't know:**
> **"Say 'I don't know, but I'll find out.' Lying or BS-ing destroys credibility permanently."**
